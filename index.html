Grammar: meaning

What do is and are tell us?

We use is and are to say who people are, how old they are, and how they are in a family.

He’s my son. He’s eight. Who is “he”? How old is he?

David. He is 8.

She’s my daughter. She’s ten. One person or more than one?

One person: Emily.

Are your children here? One child or more than one?

More than one.

Is he your husband? Statement or question?

A question. The answer is no: he’s Liz’s brother.

My husband isn’t here. Is Liz’s husband at the party?

No.
